Why this exists

Most "one-person company" material is a diagram of an aspiration. This one is a model you can run, and a set of rules that can fail.

Three structural invariants

1 · Material conservation

Every entry in the source table belongs to exactly one role slot. The ten slots sum to the table, exactly — no double counting between a theme and the channel that carries it.

2 · One area metric

Treemap area means one thing and one thing only: pieces produced per month, derived from the residual minutes left after automation. Nothing else is allowed to influence the area.

3 · Exactly one cash outlet

When off-premise delivery was discontinued, the only path to cash disappeared and the map became honest about having no revenue at all. A single cash outlet was then added, deliberately, and a validator enforces that there is exactly one.

Numbers carry their own provenance

Every figure on this page falls into one of three tiers, and the tier is stated rather than implied.

Verifiable

Counts read live from the source table. You can recount them yourself.

Declared basis

Per-step minutes are a declared model, not a stopwatch reading. Treated as an assumption to be revised, never as a measurement.

Not modelled

Revenue, conversion rates and costs are not derived from this table at all. Any income figure in this project comes from somewhere else, or from nowhere.

Four quadrants

Tooling is not automatically good. A pure-manual slot has a heavy human cost and cannot be copied; a fully automated slot has a different risk — the person stops being the bottleneck and starts being the only place judgment can live.

Risk register

A cross-section cannot show risk. Risk has to be bound to an observable indicator and a threshold, or it is just worry with a nice heading.

Material running dry
Indicator: Usable entries remaining in the primary material pool
Threshold: Remaining usable entries < 3 months of output
When material runs out you are pushed to cut quality or stop publishing, and the slot loses its supply.
Response: Widen the pool: cross-source from daily-routine, reflection and solar-term material, or split long pieces into series
Bottleneck hardening
Indicator: Share of per-piece minutes taken by the single largest step
Threshold: > 25% and the step is manual
One step eating a quarter of your attention is where the real capacity ceiling sits.
Response: Tool it or subtract from it: can the step be deleted, merged, or turned into a reusable template?
Compliance backlash
Indicator: Validator error count / injection interception rate
Threshold: Error count > 0, or interception rate < 100%
One out-of-bounds item can cost an entire channel its trust, and distribution cannot be taken back.
Response: Pause publishing, fix the validator first, then resume. Red lines get no exceptions
Capacity overdraft
Indicator: Residual minutes of weekly output as a share of available weekly hours
Threshold: > 100% (the schedule already exceeds available hours)
Running overloaded for long costs quality and eventually trips either a compliance problem or a silent stop.
Response: Lower the frequency, not the quality: move from daily to every other day and protect per-piece standards
Single-point dependency
Indicator: Number of purely manual steps in a role slot
Threshold: ≥ 2 and neither is transferable
A one-person company's capacity swings with their own state, and a single point of dependency makes the schedule wrong in a streak.
Response: Turn one of them semi-automated: the script drafts, the human only finalises
Metric drift
Indicator: Whether the area metric and tooling ratio are still traceable
Threshold: Either one can no longer be traced back to the data layer
Once the metric stops being verifiable, the map becomes self-persuasion and decisions lose their basis.
Response: Stop using the affected conclusions, go back to the SSOT, rebuild the metric, then recompute

How to read the thresholds

Every threshold is a number you can read off the table, not a feeling. If a threshold is crossed, the response is written down in advance — including the two cases where the response is to stop publishing and fix the validator first.

Metric drift is the dangerous one

The other five risks cost output. Metric drift costs the ability to make output decisions at all: once the numbers stop being traceable, the map becomes self-persuasion.

Compliance gets no exceptions

The response to a compliance breach is always the same: pause, fix, verify, resume. There is no threshold above which a red line becomes negotiable.
